# Operating QRSSTVAE on 60 m (US)

*Written 2026-10-10 against US rules in force since 2026-02-13. Not legal
advice: check the current text of 47 CFR 97.303(h) before you transmit.*

QRSSTVAE is an ultra-slow, very narrow picture mode built on SSTVAE.
SSTVAE sends a picture in about a minute across roughly 1200 Hz. QRSSTVAE
sends the same kind of compressed picture over 30 to 90 minutes on one
narrow carrier. That lets several stations share one SSB passband at
different audio offsets, and lets repeated passes add up to dig weak
pictures out of the noise.

## Recommendation

- **Dial 5352.0 kHz, USB.**
- **9 W or less into a dipole.** Use less if your antenna has gain.
- **Audio offset 1000 Hz by default**, which puts you at 5353.0 kHz RF.
  Other stations use offsets from 300 to 1900 Hz (5352.3 to 5353.9 kHz).
  That keeps every QRSSTVAE signal between 5351.5 and 5354.0 kHz, well
  clear of FT8 at a 5357.0 kHz dial.
- **Be present as control operator for every pass.** On 60 m the
  scheduler may key the rig, but somebody has to be able to stop it (see
  below).

## The rules this is based on

The FCC's Report and Order FCC 25-60 (published at 91 FR 1405,
2026-01-14, effective 2026-02-13) changed 60 m. It added a contiguous
segment from 5351.5 to 5366.5 kHz and kept four of the old channels.

| | New segment 5351.5-5366.5 kHz | Channels at 5332, 5348, 5373, 5405 kHz |
|---|---|---|
| Status | Secondary; General class and above | Secondary |
| Power | 9.15 W ERP (15 W EIRP) | 100 W ERP |
| Bandwidth | 2.8 kHz or less | 2.8 kHz |
| Modes | All modes, under the rest of Part 97 | USB voice, CW, data |
| Placement | Anywhere inside the segment; no channels | CW and data on the channel centre |

- **Use the segment, not a channel.** On the channels, data has to sit on
  the channel centre, so stations can't share one at different offsets.
  The segment has no channels. Waveform CE puts 99% of its power within
  51.5 Hz and waveform L is about 22 Hz wide, both far under 2.8 kHz.
- **Power.** ERP is the transmitter's PEP times the antenna's gain over
  a dipole. CE is constant envelope, so its PEP is just the carrier
  power. 9.15 W ERP is about 9 W into a dipole.
- **Transmission length.** Part 97 sets no limit on how long one
  transmission may last or how often you transmit. The limits that apply
  are the general ones. You may not cause wilful interference
  (97.101(d)). As a secondary user you must not cause harmful
  interference to the band's primary federal users, you must accept
  interference from them, and you must stop if asked.
- **Control operator.** 97.221(b) lists the bands where an automatically
  controlled data station may operate unattended, and 60 m isn't one of
  them. 97.221(c) covers only stations answering an interrogation. So
  every 60 m pass needs local or remote control (97.109): a control
  operator at a control point who can stop the transmission. Unattended
  overnight schedules aren't allowed on 60 m. By contrast, 10.140-10.150
  MHz on 30 m is in the 97.221(b) list.
- **Station ID.** 97.119(a) requires your callsign at least every 10
  minutes and at the end of a transmission. QRSSTVAE's four Morse ID
  windows come every 7.5 minutes, and the last one ends the pass. CE
  identifies by frequency-shift keying unless you pick on-off keying
  (`--ook`). Strictly, the CW definition in 97.3(c)(1) doesn't list
  frequency-shift keying of the carrier, and on-off keying removes that
  question.
- **Band plan (voluntary).** In the IARU plan, 5351.5-5354.0 kHz is for
  CW and narrow digital modes, 5354.0-5366.0 kHz is for all modes, and
  5366.0-5366.5 kHz is for weak signals about 20 Hz wide or less, where
  WSPR now sits. CE is too wide for that last sub-band.
- **Your radio.** Some US-model rigs only transmit on the old channel
  frequencies on 60 m. Check that yours will transmit at 5352 kHz.
